What each one is

  • parser: the shared parser reads every date month first. Our tenants write the day first, so day and month swap: a booking for 7 April arrives as 4 July. This is the bug #4471 names.
  • hint: booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants type what the hint says, and the form reads it day first. Same symptom, different code: the fix is the hint, not the parser. Only reports sent through that form at booking-v3 tenants.
  • one-day: the desk read these reports as a one-day move, not a swap, and listed them in desk-notes.json: “Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May”, “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.”, “Every booking in June is one day off.” No day/month swap does that. Our best guess is time zones; one report (“the invite arrived before I made the booking”) fits that only loosely. The desk counts these reports under parser too, where it files them by tenant; until now they had no ticket of their own, only a footnote.

Why one ticket hid them

  • The merge rule. Decision 0001 merges on the subject line. Subjects name the symptom, not the cause: “Date is wrong” and “Month and day swapped?” turn up under every problem above.
  • The intake tag. meta.intakeTag is a guess from the reporter’s browser: every en-US browser is tagged hint, every other one parser. The cause depends on the tenant, its booking form and the channel (causes.ts), so the tag often disagrees with the problem (see “tagged as” above). Don’t count by it.
  • The page said one thing, its table another. The incident page’s own table showed more than one cause, and a footnote for the one-day moves, under a callout that said “one issue”.

What changes

  • Track each problem in its own ticket (linked in the table) and close each with its own fix.
  • Merge by cause (tenant, booking form, channel), not by subject. Decision 0001 stays as written; it needs a new record to replace it.
  • Triage: duplicate reports still teaches the subject rule and is stale. Re-read it with the new record.
